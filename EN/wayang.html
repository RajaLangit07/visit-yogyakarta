Classical Wayang Performing Arts

The classical performing art of Wayang is one of the peaks of the noble cultural and civilizational achievements of Indonesia. More than an ordinary folk entertainment, wayang is a highly complex performing art entity, rich in spiritual values. On a single wayang stage, a harmonious blend shines through of craft art, drama, classical literature, and the beauty of karawitan music. Its ability to survive across many eras of civilization, from the age of ancestral beliefs, the Hindu-Buddhist kingdoms, and the spread of Islam to modern times, is proof of how strong the appeal and flexibility of this culture are. It is no wonder that UNESCO has recognized wayang as one of the world's most remarkable intangible cultural heritages, where every movement holds moral teachings, ethics, and a mirror of human life.

An Overview of Wayang

Wayang is one of the finest and oldest traditional performing arts that grew and developed in the Archipelago, especially on the islands of Java and Bali. The word "Wayang" is believed to come from the word "Bayang" or "Mewayang-wayang", which refers to the shadows cast by the white cloth screen (kelir) during a performance, or means to gaze into the spirits of the ancestors.

The art of wayang performance does not merely offer a spectacle or entertainment, but is a highly complex performing art. A wayang performance harmoniously combines many elements of art:

A. Visual Art & Craft: The making of the wayang characters, with very detailed carving (tatahan) and coloring (sungging) full of symbolism.

B. Music & Literature: The sound of Gamelan accompaniment, songs (siteran/sulukan), and classical literary language full of meaning.

C. Acting & Narration: Controlled by a Dalang (puppeteer) who acts as director, narrator, voice of dozens of characters, and the spiritual director of the performance.

Background and History of Development

The history of wayang in the Archipelago is very long and dynamic, developing along with the currents of culture and the beliefs of the people:

A. The Pre-Hindu Period (Animist & Dynamist Belief Systems)

Long before outside influences arrived, the indigenous people of the Archipelago already knew rites of worshipping ancestral spirits (hyang). In this period, the early form of wayang is thought to have been shadows or simple images used by customary/spiritual leaders to summon and communicate with the spirits of the ancestors in order to ask for protection, a blessed harvest, or the warding off of misfortune.

B. The Hindu-Buddhist Period (1st to 15th Century CE)

The arrival of Hindu-Buddhist influence from India brought the great epic stories, namely the Mahabharata and the Ramayana. These stories were not swallowed whole, but adopted and acculturated with local Javanese values (Javanization). Unique local characters such as the Punakawan (Semar, Gareng, Petruk, Bagong) were added to the Mahabharata and Ramayana epics as wise yet entertaining attendants.

C. The Islamic Period (15th to 20th Century CE)

During the era of the Islamic Kingdoms (especially the Demak Sultanate and the role of the Wali Songo, such as Sunan Kalijaga), the visual form of wayang underwent a major transformation. Wayang images that were once realistic and resembled humans were changed to be more abstract and stylized (decorative), in keeping with the teachings of Islam, which prohibit the absolute depiction of humans.

In this era, wayang was also cleverly used as a medium for Islamic preaching. Religious terms and moral teachings were subtly instilled, such as the concept of Kalimasada (symbolizing the Shahada) and terms of life philosophy.

D. The Modern Period (20th Century to the Present)

Wayang continued to survive and adapt through the colonial period and into the era of Indonesian independence. Wayang has been very well preserved in the keraton circles (Surakarta and Yogyakarta) as the standard of classical performance (pakem), while continuing to develop among the wider public as a folk performing art.

The Specialties of Wayang Performing Arts

Wayang is not merely an ordinary traditional art, but a cultural system with many extraordinary specialties:

1. The Central Role of the Dalang (An Extraordinary Multitalent)

The main specialty of a wayang performance lies in the figure of the Dalang. A dalang is a versatile solo artist (a one-man show):

- Must be able to memorize hundreds of story plays (lakon) along with the genealogies of their characters.

- Masters vocal techniques to voice dozens of characters with different intonations.

- Sets the tempo of the performance, leads dozens of gamelan musicians (niaga) and singers (pesinden), and technically controls the movements of the wayang throughout the night without long rests.

2. Rich in Philosophical Values and Life Ethics

A wayang performance is a mirror of human life (order and guidance). Wayang plays always present the eternal struggle between good (dharma) and evil (adharma).

- The Punakawan (Semar, Gareng, Petruk, Bagong): Symbolize the common people who, though seemingly simple, hold the highest moral wisdom and wise judgment.

- Each character's nature (such as Bima, who is honest and firm, Arjuna, who is calm and refined, and Duryodana, who is greedy) offers a deep moral lesson about the consequences of every deed (karma).

3. Cosmological and Spiritual Symbolism

Every element in a wayang kulit performance contains metaphysical symbolism:

- The white screen (kelir): symbolizes the universe, where the wayang shadows are manifestations of the real world, full of illusion.

- The blencong (oil lamp/light): Represents the sun, the source of life and divine light.

- The debog (the banana trunk into which the wayang are planted): Represents the earth on which we stand.

Right and Left Sides: The right-hand group represents goodness and truth, while the left-hand group represents evil and worldly desire.

4. High Adaptability and Flexibility

Although it has standard rules (pakem), wayang has a very high degree of flexibility called Lakon Gubahan or Goro-Goro. In the Goro-Goro session, the dalang can bring in current social issues, social criticism wrapped in fresh humor, humanitarian messages, and even moral education without disrupting the main storyline.

5. A Complete Synthesis of the Arts

Few performing arts in the world combine so many branches of art at once on a single stage: carving/sculpting, painting, literature, theater/dramaturgy, karawitan music, and spiritual philosophy.

The classical performing art of wayang is a cultural masterpiece of very high value. More than a shadow-art performance, wayang is a medium for character education, life philosophy, and cultural identity that has survived across the ages of civilization without losing its moral relevance for human life.
