Sate Klatak

Yogyakarta's culinary art is not only identified with sweetly seasoned dishes like gudeg or sweet snacks like bakpia, but also holds a very iconic grilled-meat masterpiece: Sate Klatak.

An Overview of Sate Klathak

Sate Klathak is a goat satay dish typical of the Imogiri area, Bantul, Yogyakarta. Unlike typical goat satay, which is associated with thick sweet soy sauce or heavy peanut sauce, sate klathak is strikingly simple yet bold.

In appearance and presentation, sate klathak has distinctive features that contrast sharply with other satay of the Archipelago:

- Minimal Seasoning: Young goat meat is seasoned only with salt (and sometimes a little coriander or garlic) before being grilled, with no sweet soy sauce brushed on during grilling.

- Iron / Bicycle Spoke Skewers: The meat is not skewered on ordinary bamboo, but on thick iron rods or bicycle wheel spokes.

- Gulai Broth as an Accompaniment: Sate klathak is served not with peanut sauce, but doused in or accompanied by a bowl of goat gulai broth that is savory, rich in spices, and yellowish in color.

The meat used is generally young goat (batibul, or under two years old) so that the fibers are tender, juicy, and free of any gamey smell.

Historical Background and Origins

The history of sate klathak is firmly rooted in the southern part of Yogyakarta, specifically in Pleret and Imogiri Districts, Bantul Regency.

A. The Culinary Pioneer (Mbah Ambyah)

The presence of sate klathak cannot be separated from its pioneer, Mbah Ambyah, who began selling goat satay around the 1940s in the Jejeran Market area of Pleret, Bantul. The Jejeran area itself has long been known as a livestock-raising area and a center for goat slaughter.

B. The Origin of the Name "Klathak"

There are two of the most popular historical versions of how the name Klathak came about:

1. The Sound of Grilling (Klathak-Klathak):When raw goat meat sprinkled with coarse salt (garam krosok) is grilled over teak wood charcoal or coconut shell charcoal, the salt grains crack and pop, making a sound like "klathak... klathak... klathak". From that distinctive sound of the grill, people called it Sate Klathak.

2. The Melinjo Fruit (Klathak): Mbah Ambyah first sold his satay under a melinjo tree (the so tree). Old melinjo fruit that has fallen to the ground is called klathak in Javanese. The place where he sold, amid the fallen melinjo fruit, was later associated with the name of the satay.

C. Evolution into a Culinary Icon of Bantul

At first, sate klathak was only popular among local Bantul residents and pilgrims heading to the Tombs of the Mataram Kings in Imogiri. But over time, the uniqueness of its flavor and cooking technique attracted food lovers from outside the region and even from abroad, making the East Imogiri Road area the leading center of sate klathak in Yogyakarta.

The Specialties of Sate Klathak

Sate klathak has a series of technical and flavor specialties that give it a special place on the culinary map of Indonesia:

1. The Innovation of Iron Rod Skewers (Heat Conductors)

The most prominent specialty of sate klathak is the use of bicycle spokes or iron rods in place of bamboo skewers. Using iron is not just for show, but serves a very crucial scientific function:

- Even Cooking All the Way Through: Iron is an excellent heat conductor. While the outside of the meat is grilled by the charcoal, the iron skewer inside the meat heats up too, so the meat is cooked perfectly from both the outside and the inside at the same time.

- Preserving the Signature Texture: Because the inside and outside cook in parallel, the meat does not have to be grilled too long until it burns. As a result, the meat stays tender and juicy, and its fibers do not turn tough or chewy.

2. The Boldness of Salt Seasoning (Respecting the Quality of the Meat)

While most satay dishes rely on the caramelization of sweet soy sauce or the savoriness of peanut sauce to cover the typical goat smell, sate klathak uses only salt. This specialty shows pride in the quality of the goat meat used. The salt that soaks in actually brings out the natural savory taste (umami) and the true deliciousness of the succulence of young goat meat.

3. The Combination of Satay and Gulai Broth

The way sate klathak is eaten gives a unique taste experience. Pieces of the savory-salty satay meat are dipped in or doused with gulai broth that is savory, fragrant with spices (such as cardamom, cloves, cinnamon, and coriander), and lightly made with thin coconut milk. The combination of the plain savory grilled meat with the richness of the gulai spices creates a very rich harmony of savory flavor on the tongue.

4. A Hearty Portion of Meat

The pieces of meat in sate klathak are generally much larger than in ordinary goat satay. Usually, one serving of sate klathak consists of only two iron skewers, but each skewer holds thick, satisfying pieces of meat.

Sate Klathak Jogja is a reflection of simplicity that gives birth to deep deliciousness. By using the clever technique of grilling on iron rods and a minimalist salt seasoning, sate klathak proves that the true deliciousness of a dish does not always come from complex seasoning, but from the quality of the main ingredient and the right cooking technique.
