Prambanan Temple: The Grandeur of a Hindu Masterpiece in Java

A magnificent 9th-century Hindu temple complex with tall, soaring towers. One of the most beautiful architectural legacies in Indonesia.

General Overview

Prambanan Temple, also known as Roro Jonggrang Temple, is the largest Hindu (Shiva) temple complex in Indonesia and one of the most magnificent temples in Southeast Asia. Located on the border between Sleman Regency, Special Region of Yogyakarta, and Klaten Regency, Central Java, this temple complex stands gracefully as a symbol of the glory of ancient Javanese civilization.

Architecturally, Prambanan Temple follows the typical Hindu building layout with tall, slender forms. Its main building rises 47 meters in the middle of the courtyard complex, surrounded by hundreds of accompanying temples (perwara). In 1991, UNESCO officially designated Prambanan Temple a World Heritage Site.

Historical Background

Prambanan Temple is thought to have been built in the mid-9th century CE (around 850 CE) by Rakai Pikatan, a king of the Sanjaya Dynasty who ruled the Ancient Mataram Kingdom. The temple was built to:

Honor the Trimurti:

Serve as a place of worship and offerings to the three main gods of Hinduism, namely Brahma (the Creator), Vishnu (the Preserver), and Shiva (the Destroyer).

Mark the Rise of the Sanjaya Dynasty:

The temple was built as a counterpart to Borobudur Temple (a Buddhist temple) built by the Shailendra Dynasty. Its construction reaffirmed the Sanjaya Dynasty's dominance in Central Java and the Special Region of Yogyakarta.

Serve as a Royal Religious Center:

Prambanan functioned as the site of official royal religious ceremonies and a center of spiritual activity.

However, a few decades after being completed and expanded by later kings (such as Balitung Maha Sambu), the complex began to be abandoned around the 10th century because of the relocation of the Ancient Mataram Kingdom's center to East Java by Mpu Sindok, which is thought to have been triggered by a great eruption of Mount Merapi and other natural disasters. The temple ruins lay buried under soil and vegetation for centuries until they were finally rediscovered during the colonial period.

The Specialties and Uniqueness of Prambanan Temple

A. Impressive Silpasastra-Style Architecture

The layout of Prambanan Temple was designed based on the Silpasastra (a Hindu architectural guide). The temple buildings are divided into three zones that symbolize the hierarchy of the universe (Triloka):

  • Lower Zone (Bhurloka): The courtyard area and accompanying temples, symbolizing the human world.
  • Middle Zone (Bhuvarloka): The main temples, symbolizing the world of the gods.
  • Upper Zone (Svarloka): The temple peaks, symbolizing the highest spiritual world.

B. The Trimurti Temple Complex and Vehicle Temples

In the main courtyard (the core zone), three main temples stand majestically:

  1. Shiva Temple: The largest and tallest temple (47 meters) in the middle, which houses statues of Shiva Mahadeva, Agastya, Ganesha, and Durga Mahisasuramardini (who in folklore is linked to the legend of Roro Jonggrang).
  2. Vishnu Temple & Brahma Temple: Two temples that flank the Shiva Temple to the north and south.
  3. Vehicle (Wahana) Temples: Three temples facing the main temples, dedicated to the gods' mounts: Nandi (the bull, Shiva's mount), Garuda (the bird, Vishnu's mount), and the Swan (Brahma's mount).

C. Relief Masterpieces of the Ramayana and Krishnayana Epics

The outer balustrade walls of the Shiva Temple and Brahma Temple are decorated with carved stone reliefs telling the Ramayana epic, from the abduction of Princess Sita by Ravana to the great war and Sita's return. Meanwhile, the reliefs on the Vishnu Temple depict the Krishnayana epic. These reliefs are carved in remarkable detail and are considered among the most beautiful stone carving works of their time.

D. The Ramayana Ballet Performance Stage

A modern specialty of Prambanan Temple is its open-air theatre where the Ramayana Ballet is regularly performed. Set against the magnificent backdrop of the three main temples lit up at night, this colossal dance without dialogue unites dance, gamelan music, and history at the original site.

E. Acculturation and the Legendary Folk Tale

Prambanan is not only rich in historical value, but is also deeply rooted in Javanese folklore through the Legend of Roro Jonggrang. The story of Bandung Bondowoso, who was asked to build 1,000 temples in one night in order to win Roro Jonggrang's hand, is a cultural attraction in itself that continues to be told from generation to generation.

Prambanan Temple is the largest masterpiece of Hindu architecture and carving in Indonesia, built in the 9th century by the Sanjaya Dynasty as a tribute to the Trimurti gods. Not only a symbol of religion and of the glory of the Ancient Mataram Kingdom, Prambanan also has extraordinary specialties in its Triloka-concept layout, the detailed reliefs of the Ramayana epic, and the blend of history and the folk legend of Roro Jonggrang. Its designation as a UNESCO World Heritage Site further confirms that Prambanan Temple is a priceless historical legacy for world civilization.
